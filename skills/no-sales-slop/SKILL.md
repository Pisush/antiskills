# No sales slop

The reader gets many emails like yours every week and deletes most of them within seconds. Every rule below comes from a pattern that got a real pitch deleted.

## Facts

- **Do not recite the reader's own website back to them.** Quoting their attendee numbers or schedule shows you scraped a page, not that you care. Use a fact only if it explains why your product fits.
- **Check every fact before you use it.** Wrong year, wrong edition, a domain they do not own, a role they do not have ("you make videos about AI tools"). One wrong fact ends the read.
- **Do not claim you watched, read or used their work** unless you did and can say something specific about it.
- **Remove template seams.** Placeholders, notes to yourself ("date to be checked"), and the wrong language for the reader are all visible.

## The ask

- **Say what the product does in one plain sentence near the top.** Not the vision, not the roadmap.
- **Do not sell what is not built.** Features "in development" are not features.
- **State the price, or say it is free and what the catch is.** A fee hidden in the fourth sentence reads as a trick.
- **Do not ask for a call or demo as the first step.** Give something the reader can judge in one minute without talking to you: a link, a price, one example.
- **Do not ask the reader to find the right person for you.** "Who should I talk to?" hands them your work.
- **Do not ask for free work.** "Free access" in exchange for testing, feedback, referrals or promotion is a request for unpaid work. Say so plainly, or offer to pay.
- **Do not turn their audience into your revenue.** Offering to charge their attendees is not a partnership offer.

## Proof

- **Do not use social proof the reader cannot check.** Big brand names, follower counts and "seven figures" without a link read as invented.
- **Do not send a "personal video".** Nobody believes it was recorded for them.
- **Do not open with flattery about one feature of theirs.** It reads as the hook it is.
- **Do not invent a problem.** "I am not sure this is a problem for you, but the industry average is..." admits you do not know.

## Tone and form

- **Address a person or the team by name.** "Hi there", "Dear Sir/Madam" and greeting the event as if it were a person all say "mass mail".
- **No pressure devices.** "Last one from me", "bumping this up", "you might have missed", a lone "^ any interest?".
- **Do not make opting out cost a reply.** "Just reply 'no'" confirms the address is live. Offer an unsubscribe link, or simply do not follow up.
- **One follow-up at most,** and only if it adds new information.
- **Subject lines say what it is.** Not "Quick question", "An idea for you", or a bare event name.

## Before sending

1. Is every fact about the reader correct and current?
2. Can the reader tell in one sentence what it is and what it costs?
3. Can they decide without a call?
4. Is anything in it unpaid work for them?
5. Would you send it if your name, not a sales team, were on it?

If any answer is no, fix it or do not send.
